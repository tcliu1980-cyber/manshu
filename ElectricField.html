Electric Field and Electric Force

An electric field (E) is a vector field created by source charges. When a test charge (q) is placed within this field, it experiences an electrostatic force (F). The field exists independently of the test charge, acting as the mediator of the electric interaction.

The relationship between the electric force and the electric field is given by the equation:

F = q · E

Where:

  • F is the net electrostatic force vector experienced by the test charge.
  • q is the magnitude and sign of the test charge.
  • E is the net electric field vector at the location of the test charge, produced by all other source charges.

Notice that for a positive test charge, the force vector points in the exact same direction as the electric field. For a negative test charge, the force vector points in the opposite direction of the electric field.
